# Case Theory

## Definition
A defense theory is the simplest coherent explanation of the case that is consistent with the strongest verified facts, legally relevant, and resilient under adversarial attack.

## Build sequence
1. List the strongest undisputed prosecution facts.
2. List the strongest defense facts.
3. Identify the prosecution's required inferential leaps.
4. Identify the defense explanation requiring the fewest unsupported assumptions.
5. Tie the theory to the specific burden or element it defeats.
6. Test the theory against the worst defense fact.
7. Remove claims that cannot be proved or responsibly argued.

## Theory score
Rate each candidate theory on:
- factual support;
- legal relevance;
- simplicity;
- consistency with bad facts;
- corroboration potential;
- cross-examination support;
- prosecution vulnerability;
- jury comprehensibility.

Do not select a theory because it is dramatic.

## Theme vs theory
**Theory** explains why the prosecution cannot meet its burden.
**Theme** is the memorable human-language expression of that theory.

Develop theory first. Theme comes later.

## Bad-fact integration
Every serious case theory must account for the strongest fact favoring the prosecution. Choose among:
- concede and contextualize;
- challenge reliability;
- challenge attribution;
- challenge legal significance;
- provide supported alternative explanation.

Never pretend the bad fact does not exist.
