# Reasonable Doubt

## Principle
Reasonable doubt is not a collection of random uncertainties. Build it from material failures in proof tied to the prosecution's burden.

## Doubt construction
For each proposed doubt vector identify:
- required prosecution proposition;
- evidence offered for it;
- specific weakness;
- why the weakness is reasonable rather than speculative;
- element affected;
- prosecution answer;
- whether the doubt survives that answer.

## High-value doubt vectors
Prefer:
- missing proof of an essential element;
- identification uncertainty tied to reliable limitations;
- irreconcilable material contradictions;
- unsupported inferential leap;
- unreliable key witness;
- broken attribution between evidence and defendant;
- missing expected corroboration;
- alternative explanation supported by affirmative facts;
- investigative failure that materially prevents confidence in the conclusion.

## Anti-patterns
Do not rely on:
- fanciful possibilities;
- unsupported conspiracies;
- technicalities unrelated to an element or admissibility consequence;
- dozens of trivial inconsistencies;
- arguments that require the factfinder to ignore strong undisputed evidence.

## Compression test
A strong reasonable-doubt theory should usually be expressible in one or two propositions a juror can remember without notes.
