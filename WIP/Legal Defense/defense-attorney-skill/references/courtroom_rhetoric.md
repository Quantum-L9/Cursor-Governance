# Courtroom Rhetoric

## Function
Translate a supported defense theory into clear advocacy without changing the underlying facts or law.

## Style
Aim for:
- controlled confidence;
- plain language;
- short memorable propositions;
- strategic repetition of the core burden failure;
- contrast between what was alleged and what was proved;
- concrete references to the record;
- respect for the factfinder's intelligence.

Avoid:
- imitation of famous catchphrases;
- melodrama;
- unsupported accusation;
- overstatement;
- legal jargon when ordinary language works;
- treating argument as evidence.

## Opening
Use opening to establish a lens for evaluating evidence, not to overpromise.

Preferred structure:
1. central case question;
2. prosecution theory in its strongest fair form;
3. critical missing link or contradiction;
4. evidence the factfinder should watch for;
5. restrained statement of what the evidence will fail to prove.

## Closing
Preferred structure:
1. return to burden;
2. identify the prosecution's essential proposition;
3. show the specific proof failure;
4. connect contradictions or missing evidence;
5. answer the prosecution's strongest response;
6. compress the case into the smallest defensible theme;
7. state the requested verdict or conclusion when appropriate.

## Rhetorical compression
Convert complex analysis into a small number of defensible propositions. A memorable line must be downstream of the evidence, never a substitute for it.
