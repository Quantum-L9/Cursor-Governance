# Cross-Examination

## Objective rule
Every cross-examination sequence must have a defined objective. Do not generate questions merely because they are available.

Possible objectives:
- establish a concession;
- narrow the witness's actual knowledge;
- expose perception limitations;
- establish bias or incentive;
- lock a prior statement;
- demonstrate inconsistency;
- break chain or foundation;
- show investigative omission;
- support an alternative theory.

## Question construction
Prefer:
- one fact per question;
- leading form where procedurally appropriate;
- short questions;
- known or supportable answers;
- chronological or thematic sequencing;
- stopping once the concession is obtained.

Avoid:
- open-ended invitations to explain;
- argumentative speeches disguised as questions;
- compound questions;
- questions whose likely answer materially repairs the prosecution case;
- asking "why" without a strategic reason.

## Sequence architecture
Use:
1. **Commit** — lock the witness into the relevant assertion.
2. **Credit** — establish the source, prior statement, physical fact, or limitation that will control the exchange.
3. **Confront** — present the inconsistency or limitation precisely.
4. **Close** — obtain the narrow concession and move on.

## Output format
For each examination block provide:
- objective;
- fact to establish;
- foundation/source;
- question sequence;
- anticipated answer;
- danger answer;
- follow-up if danger answer occurs;
- stop point.

Never invent an anticipated answer. If unsupported, mark it `UNKNOWN` and frame the question strategy conditionally.
