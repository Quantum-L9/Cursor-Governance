# Evidence Analysis

## Evidence-first rule
Analyze evidence as a chain of propositions, not as a label. "DNA," "video," "phone records," "confession," or "eyewitness" are categories, not conclusions.

## Evidence matrix
For each item identify:
- item/source;
- proposition offered;
- offense element affected;
- authenticity foundation;
- attribution to defendant;
- collection method;
- preservation/chain issues;
- completeness/context;
- corroboration;
- contradiction;
- alternative explanation;
- admissibility issue;
- defense value;
- unresolved question.

## Reliability attack
Ask in order:
1. Is it what the proponent says it is?
2. Was it collected and preserved reliably?
3. Does it actually connect to the defendant?
4. Does it prove the claimed proposition?
5. Does the proposition prove an element?
6. Is there omitted context that changes meaning?
7. What error, contamination, bias, or alternative explanation remains plausible?

## Digital evidence
Treat timestamps, account ownership, device possession, IP attribution, location data, deleted material, screenshots, exports, and message authorship as separate propositions. Do not treat access to an account or device as automatic proof of authorship or intent.

## Forensic/scientific evidence
Separate:
- sample integrity;
- method validity;
- laboratory execution;
- analyst interpretation;
- statistical meaning;
- transfer/secondary-transfer possibilities;
- connection to the charged event.

Do not state scientific reliability beyond the supplied evidence or verified authoritative source.

## Statements and confessions
Examine:
- exact wording;
- recording completeness;
- interrogation context;
- voluntariness issues;
- ambiguity;
- contamination by interviewer facts;
- consistency with independently known facts;
- whether claimed admissions are direct or interpretive paraphrases.

## Missing-evidence test
Ask what evidence would ordinarily be expected if the prosecution narrative were accurate. Absence matters only when there is a sound reason that evidence should exist and be obtainable.
