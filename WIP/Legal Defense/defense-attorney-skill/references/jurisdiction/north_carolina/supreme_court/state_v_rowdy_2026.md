## Holding encoded for use
The Court upheld, under the record presented, a **Terry frisk** based on reasonable suspicion and a **warrantless vehicle search** based on probable cause. These are distinct police actions governed by distinct standards.

The official headnote identifies relevant circumstances including:
- failure to promptly pull over in response to blue lights and sirens;
- presence in an area characterized in the record as high-crime;
- marijuana odor from the vehicle;
- evasive behavior during questioning;
- prior criminal history; and
- subsequent discovery of a suspected marijuana blunt on Rowdy's person before the vehicle-search probable-cause determination described in the headnote.

## Mandatory separation
Never collapse these inquiries:

### Terry frisk
Ask whether articulable facts supported reasonable suspicion relevant to the protective frisk at the moment it occurred.

### Vehicle search
Separately ask whether the facts known before the automobile search established probable cause that evidence of criminal activity would be found in the vehicle.

## Defense application
- isolate which facts existed before the frisk and before the vehicle search;
- challenge conclusory reliance on "high-crime area" without record support;
- challenge evasiveness characterizations against objective conduct;
- prevent prior criminal history from becoming propensity reasoning;
- distinguish cases lacking delayed stop, corroborating behavior, contraband, or other Rowdy circumstances;
- attack any attempt to bootstrap evidence found during an unlawful earlier act into justification for a later search.

## Prosecution application
Expect the State to argue that the listed factors collectively supported reasonable suspicion for the frisk and, with the additional facts available before the vehicle search, probable cause for the automobile search.

## Anti-overread
MUST NOT state:
- high-crime area alone creates reasonable suspicion;
- criminal history alone creates reasonable suspicion or probable cause;
- marijuana odor alone necessarily creates probable cause;
- reasonable suspicion for a frisk automatically authorizes an evidentiary search of a vehicle.
