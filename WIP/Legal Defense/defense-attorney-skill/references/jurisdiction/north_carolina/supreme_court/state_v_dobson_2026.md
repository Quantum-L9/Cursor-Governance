# State v. Dobson (2026)

- Author: Justice Anita Earls
- Publication: Published
- Status in pack: `CONTROLLING`, subject to later-authority verification
- Pack verified through: 2026-08-12

## Issue
Whether officers had probable cause under the Fourth Amendment to conduct a warrantless search in connection with a traffic stop.

## Holding encoded for use
The Court upheld the challenged warrantless searches under a **totality-of-the-circumstances** analysis. Marijuana odor was treated as one relevant factor rather than a stand-alone automatic rule.

The official 2026 headnote identifies relevant circumstances including:
- excessive vehicle speed providing lawful basis for the stop;
- marijuana odor;
- a cover scent officers associated with masking marijuana;
- occupants' criminal histories; and
- a background check showing serious pending charges.

The handgun visible on the dashboard was not treated as supporting probable cause because the driver identified herself as a probation and parole officer. The remaining circumstances collectively supported the inference that evidence of criminal activity would be found.

## Defense application
Do not cite Dobson merely for the proposition that odor authorizes a search. Instead:
1. identify every circumstance the State relies on;
2. remove circumstances absent from the present case;
3. challenge facts that were not known before the search;
4. challenge officer-specific interpretations such as an alleged masking scent;
5. show why the remaining totality is materially weaker than Dobson when supported by the record.

## Prosecution application
Expect reliance on Dobson for the proposition that odor can remain relevant within a broader totality even where lawful hemp/cannabis-related ambiguity exists, if additional circumstances collectively support a fair probability that evidence of crime will be found.

## Anti-overread
MUST NOT state:
- marijuana odor automatically creates probable cause;
- Dobson adopted an odor-alone categorical rule;
- every criminal-history fact carries the same weight;
- facts discovered after a search can retroactively justify that search.
