# North Carolina Criminal Procedure Core

## Trial divisions
North Carolina has District Court and Superior Court trial divisions.

### District Court
- hears misdemeanor and infraction trials;
- conducts preliminary proceedings in felony matters;
- handles first-appearance and probable-cause functions as provided by Chapter 15A, subject to applicable exceptions and local lawful procedure.

### Superior Court
- hears all felony trials;
- hears misdemeanor and infraction appeals from District Court;
- conducts felony proceedings after indictment/bind-over as authorized by law.

## Procedural routing
Before analysis, classify:
- offense class: felony / misdemeanor / infraction / UNKNOWN;
- stage: investigation / charging / initial appearance / first appearance / probable-cause hearing / indictment / arraignment / pretrial motions / plea / trial / sentencing / appeal / post-conviction;
- court: District / Superior / appellate / UNKNOWN;
- county and district;
- charging instrument;
- custody/release status when relevant.

## First appearance
N.C.G.S. § 15A-601 governs first appearance before a district court judge and related authorized first appearances. Do not assume a generic timetable when a current statute or valid local order changes the operative scheduling mechanics.

N.C.G.S. §§ 15A-602 through 15A-605 govern duties associated with first appearance, including advice concerning counsel and charges and release-related determinations.

## Probable-cause hearing
Under N.C.G.S. § 15A-606, a defendant charged with an offense in the original jurisdiction of Superior Court generally receives a scheduled probable-cause hearing unless properly waived. Verify the current statute and procedural posture before asserting entitlement or deadline.

## Arraignment
N.C.G.S. §§ 15A-941 through 15A-945 govern Superior Court arraignment mechanics. Section 15A-943 contains required calendaring rules for covered counties/sessions.

## Venue
N.C.G.S. § 15A-131 and related provisions govern criminal venue. Do not equate county location with subject-matter jurisdiction or assume venue is uncontested.
