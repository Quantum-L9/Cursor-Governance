# North Carolina Authority Hierarchy

## Use
Apply this file before making a North Carolina legal conclusion.

## Hierarchy
For a North Carolina criminal case, analyze authority in this order, subject to issue-specific supremacy and jurisdiction rules:

1. **U.S. Constitution and controlling U.S. Supreme Court precedent** for federal constitutional questions.
2. **North Carolina Constitution** for independent state constitutional questions.
3. **Supreme Court of North Carolina published decisions** on North Carolina law.
4. **North Carolina General Statutes** and valid enacted law, read consistently with controlling constitutional authority.
5. **North Carolina Court of Appeals precedent** according to its current precedential status.
6. **Statewide rules, administrative rules, and applicable court rules** within their lawful scope.
7. **Local administrative orders and local rules** for the applicable district/county, within their lawful scope.
8. **Persuasive authority** only when controlling authority does not resolve the issue.

Do not mechanically apply the numbered order where federal supremacy, constitutional invalidity, statutory text, or a later controlling decision changes the result. Identify the actual conflict and controlling rule.

## Status labels
Use one of:
- `CONTROLLING`
- `BINDING_WITHIN_SCOPE`
- `PERSUASIVE`
- `DISTINGUISHABLE`
- `LIMITED`
- `SUPERSEDED`
- `STATUS_UNKNOWN`

## Verification protocol
Before relying on a case:
1. confirm court, docket, filing date, and publication status;
2. state the precise issue and holding;
3. identify the material facts supporting the holding;
4. check later authority when research access exists;
5. distinguish holding from dicta, digest language, concurrence, and dissent;
6. compare the current record to the material facts;
7. state the strongest defense and prosecution readings.

## Source note
North Carolina Judicial Branch describes the Supreme Court as the state's highest court with no further appeal on matters of state law and the Court of Appeals as the intermediate appellate court.
