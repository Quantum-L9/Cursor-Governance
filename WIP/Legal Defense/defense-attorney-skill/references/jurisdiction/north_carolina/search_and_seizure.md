# North Carolina Search and Seizure

## Core method
For every suppression issue, split the police encounter into discrete events. Never use one generalized label such as "the search."

Analyze, as applicable:
1. encounter / show of authority;
2. seizure or traffic stop;
3. duration and mission of detention;
4. extension of detention;
5. frisk of person;
6. search of person;
7. vehicle search;
8. container search;
9. seizure of evidence;
10. statements or derivative evidence.

For each event identify:
- governing constitutional provision;
- required legal standard;
- facts known to the officer at that moment;
- facts learned only later and therefore unavailable to justify the earlier act;
- asserted exception to warrant requirement;
- defense attack;
- State's strongest response.

## Cannabis / marijuana odor after Dobson and Rowdy
Do not apply `odor = automatic probable cause`.

The Supreme Court of North Carolina's 22 May 2026 published decisions in **State v. Dobson, 190PA24**, and **State v. Rowdy, 300PA24**, require totality-of-the-circumstances analysis on the records presented. Odor is a relevant factor, not a substitute for identifying the remaining circumstances and the legal standard applicable to the specific police action.

### Mandatory questions
- What exactly did the officer claim to smell?
- From where and at what point in the encounter?
- Was the claimed odor raw, burnt, generalized, or otherwise described?
- What other circumstances existed before the challenged action?
- Which circumstances are innocent standing alone?
- Which were supported by objective evidence?
- Which facts arose only after the frisk/search?
- Is the challenged act a Terry frisk, evidentiary search of a person, vehicle search, or other seizure?
- Does the State rely on reasonable suspicion or probable cause?
- Is the current record materially weaker or stronger than Dobson or Rowdy?

## Defense use
- atomize the State's totality into its component facts;
- challenge unsupported officer interpretations;
- remove facts learned after the challenged act from the justification timeline;
- distinguish innocent facts from facts genuinely increasing probability of criminal activity;
- compare missing corroboration against Dobson/Rowdy;
- challenge conflation of officer-safety reasonable suspicion with probable cause to search for evidence.

## State counter
Expect the State to argue that otherwise innocent factors may collectively create reasonable suspicion or probable cause and that courts assess the totality rather than each factor in isolation.
