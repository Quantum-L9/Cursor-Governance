# Legal Research Protocol

## Objective
Produce current, primary-source-grounded North Carolina criminal-defense analysis without mistaking summaries, stale case cards, or the user's working documents for controlling law.

## Mandatory sequence
1. Identify the exact legal proposition being relied on.
2. Identify jurisdiction, court, and current procedural phase.
3. Locate the highest available primary authority.
4. For statutes, verify current enacted text and effective-date relevance.
5. For cases, verify court, disposition, publication status, mandate/certification when relevant, and any later history that changes the proposition.
6. For Catawba procedure, verify the current local rule/order or statewide eCourts guidance.
7. Compare the material facts of precedent to the current facts rather than citing the case name as a conclusion.
8. Distinguish the law as of the challenged police action from later changes when temporal law matters.
9. Record a freshness date or mark status `UNKNOWN`.

## Research-output fields
For each material authority capture:
- citation / docket
- court
- date
- source type
- proposition
- precise holding or operative statutory rule
- defense use
- State use
- limits / distinguishing facts
- verification date
- subsequent-history status
- primary source locator

## No-tool mode
If live research is unavailable:
- use bundled materials only as a snapshot;
- state the bundled verification date when currentness matters;
- do not assert that an authority remains controlling if subsequent history is unknown and material;
- create a `PRIMARY_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` item rather than fabricating certainty.

## Quotation discipline
Do not quote an authority from memory. Use exact text only from a source actually available in the current session. Paraphrase otherwise.
