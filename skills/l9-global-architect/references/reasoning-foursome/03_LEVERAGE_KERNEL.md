# L9 Leverage Kernel

**Status:** Converged four-kernel baseline  
**Version:** 1.1.0  
**Semantic owner:** systemic leverage evaluation, compounding, rank, score, fingerprint, and mesh-value improvement  
**Normative law:** `references/L9_LEVERAGE_LAW_AND_RANKING_INVARIANTS_v1.0.md`

## 1. Cardinal Question

> **How does this intervention increase the constellation's capacity to make current and future participants more capable, and how does realized value compound into still more leverage?**

The Leverage Kernel evaluates value. It does not own causal selection, consequence modeling, or signal routing.

It consumes:

- the First-Order intervention;
- Second-Order predicted and observed consequence surfaces;
- Signal Leverage topology/evidence receipts where propagation matters; and
- current constellation evidence.

It determines whether the resulting architecture creates net systemic advantage and what validated gains should become reusable.

## 2. Constitutional Law

> **Everything L9 builds, adopts, learns, changes, or retains MUST increase the constellation's capacity to make its other parts and future parts more capable.**

Every participant MUST be a leverage multiplier or necessary substrate for a higher-order multiplier that cannot be achieved more simply.

## 3. Leverage Kernel Invariants

### LK-01: Consume, do not duplicate, First-Order causality
The governing intervention comes from First-Order. Leverage may challenge systemic value but MUST return intervention reconsideration to First-Order.

### LK-02: Predicted leverage MUST be grounded in Second-Order prediction
No consequence evidence means no justified leverage forecast.

### LK-03: Realized leverage MUST be grounded in observed consequences
Intent and architecture diagrams are not realized leverage.

### LK-04: Available, predicted, realized, and compounded leverage MUST remain distinct
Forecast error is learning and MUST remain visible.

### LK-05: Every significant participant MUST justify net leverage
Dependencies, tools, agents, repositories, contracts, schemas, and retained complexity must multiply useful capability or enable a higher-order multiplier that cannot be achieved more simply.

### LK-06: Validated shared gains MUST identify eligible beneficiaries
Leverage owns the judgment that a realized gain is reusable and which classes of consumers can benefit. It does NOT own the routing mechanics. Eligible dissemination is handed to Signal Leverage.

### LK-07: Realized leverage MUST be capable of changing a future baseline
Storage, reporting, or observation alone does not compound.

### LK-08: Leverage topology value MUST consume Signal Leverage evidence
One-way, bidirectional, closed-loop, mesh, and constellation-compounding structures have different leverage potential. Signal Leverage owns topology semantics and propagation receipts; Leverage owns the systemic value assigned to evidenced topology.

### LK-09: Productive subloops MAY increase leverage
A subloop has leverage value only when it produces useful changed state, reusable learning, reduced future cost, or stronger capability. Loop correctness and convergence belong to Signal Leverage.

### LK-10: Local optimization MUST NOT reduce systemic leverage
Local benefit is invalid when it materially reduces interoperability, optionality, learning capacity, maintainability, ownership clarity, propagation quality, or future leverage.

### LK-11: Net leverage MUST account for drag
Maintenance, coupling, complexity, latency, failure surface, duplicated ownership, signal noise, and cognitive burden reduce realized leverage.

### LK-12: Rank and score MUST remain distinct
Rank describes strongest evidenced leverage topology participation. Score describes realized effectiveness inside the current architecture.

### LK-13: Rank promotion MUST require evidenced systemic advantage
More edges, callbacks, fan-out, or loops do not create rank by themselves. Signal topology receipts are necessary evidence, not sufficient value proof.

### LK-14: Realized learning SHOULD strengthen future reasoning
Leverage learning may improve future First-Order judgment, Second-Order prediction, Signal Leverage routing, mesh structure, shared capabilities, and participant fingerprints.

### LK-15: Hard law outranks leverage optimization
Correctness, security, scope, semantic ownership, mandatory contracts, and higher-authority invariants cannot be sacrificed to maximize leverage.

## 6. Leverage Rank

The normative ladder remains:

- LR-0: negative or dead-weight leverage
- LR-1: local multiplier
- LR-2: bidirectional multiplier
- LR-3: closed-loop multiplier
- LR-4: multi-directional mesh multiplier
- LR-5: constellation compounding multiplier

Signal Leverage provides the topology evidence. Leverage determines whether the topology produces sufficient systemic value to justify the rank.

## 7. Leverage Score

Leverage Score is a first-class 0-100 primitive grounded in realized evidence. It MUST NOT be derived from activity theater, connection count, fan-out, token count, file count, or architectural ambition.

## 8. Failure Modes

- **LK-F01 Cause theft:** Leverage replaces First-Order intervention ownership.
- **LK-F02 Consequence invention:** Leverage invents effects not supported by Second-Order.
- **LK-F03 Routing theft:** Leverage directly owns propagation mechanics instead of handing them to Signal Leverage.
- **LK-F04 Prediction collapse:** predicted leverage is presented as realized leverage.
- **LK-F05 Connectivity theater:** rank rises because of meaningless topology.
- **LK-F06 Local-value tunnel vision:** local output is mistaken for constellation value.
- **LK-F07 Dead learning:** outcomes never alter future behavior.
- **LK-F08 Metric gaming:** score optimization reduces actual systemic value.

## 9. Relationship to the Foursome

Leverage answers:

> **What systemic value should these consequences create?**

and later:

> **What systemic value was realized, how did it compound, and which validated gains should become reusable?**

It does NOT answer:

- governing cause or intervention selection -> First-Order
- predicted/observed consequence surface -> Second-Order
- signal routing, topology correctness, branch/fan-out/reconvergence, or write-back mechanics -> Signal Leverage
